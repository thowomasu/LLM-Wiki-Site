Can Pulse Tests Replace Missing EIS Measurements?

A physics-first summary of what was tested, what worked, what failed, and what should happen next.

OverviewVersions 1 to 3Version 4Version 5Version 6Compare all versions
Answer in one sentence: the method can work when two datasets are closely matched, but it is not reliable across different cells and test methods.

What this study tried to do

An EIS test shows how a battery responds at different time scales.

EIS is useful, but it needs special equipment and takes extra test time.

A pulse test is faster and easier to collect.

This study asked whether a pulse test could replace a missing EIS test.

It also asked whether the estimated EIS could support a State of Health estimate.

Why transfer between datasets is difficult

Each dataset came from a different experimental domain.

The cell chemistry, cell design, temperature, ageing history, instrument, and test protocol were not always the same.

These differences can change the impedance spectrum even when two cells have similar health.

A model can therefore learn the habits of one experiment instead of learning a general battery relation.

What the study found

Some EIS features can be estimated

The model found useful links between pulse behaviour and parts of the impedance spectrum.

The result does not transfer broadly

The links changed when the cell family or measurement procedure changed.

Health prediction is the weaker step

An estimated spectrum did not give a reliable health result across all datasets.

A larger model is not the answer

The main limit is physical mismatch between experiments, not a lack of model complexity.

How the conclusion changed

The three decisive results

Each version tested a different part of the idea.

The charts therefore use different units and should not be combined into one score.

Versions 1 to 3

The broad idea did not hold

The first models worked inside familiar data. They failed, or could not be tested fairly, when the dataset changed.

One narrow success

Three Oxford cells passed the fixed test. The Oxford experiment was closely matched to the training data. The health step still used a measured EIS spectrum.

The complete chain failed outside its domain

The model estimated EIS before estimating health. Its safety rule rejected all 892 measurements from the outside SiCWell dataset.

Version 6

Calibration did not rescue the broad model

Several input forms, small calibration sets, safety rules, and uncertainty estimates were tested. No candidate worked reliably across every dataset.

Next study

Match the physics before fitting a model

Use one cell model, one production lot, one temperature, one ageing plan, and fixed pulse and EIS procedures. First show that ageing changes are larger than measurement noise.

Compare all versions

What should happen next

  1. Confirm that enough identical cells and the required equipment are available.
  2. Measure two cells repeatedly to quantify normal measurement variation.
  3. Continue only if the change caused by ageing is larger than that variation.
  4. Use different cells for model development, calibration, and the final test.

Current status: the new experiment is paused.

The required cells, equipment, safety approval, and schedule have not yet been confirmed.

Claim boundary: the study does not show that pulse measurements can replace EIS for every battery. It shows one narrow success and several clear limits.
