Predicting Missing Battery Measurements From Partial Test Data

A frozen V5 research method for estimating a current EIS spectrum and SOH from a fresh reference spectrum plus a matched pulse-resistance change. Its external test failed, so it is not a generally validated method.

Development results SiCWell result Domain diagnostic

Bottom line

V5 passed its internal development gate on eight held-out Leonardi cells. It then failed its frozen external test on SiCWell: the domain guard accepted 0 of 892 rows, so no external predictive score was released. Current verdict: external_validation_failed, no support.

Blunt boundary: V5 is not generally validated. A prediction is an estimate, not an observed battery measurement.

Inputs

A fresh EIS reference spectrum, a matched pulse-resistance change, and state of charge.

Outputs

Estimated current EIS, chained SOH, uncertainty intervals, and a refusal status.

Internal result

All 8 held-out Leonardi cells passed the chained SOH development gate.

External result

SiCWell was outside the frozen domain, so every row was refused.

Inputs and outputs

For each battery row, V5 needs a fresh same-cell EIS spectrum at 0.1 to 40 Hz, state of charge, and the percentage change in pulse resistance from a matched 5 to 10 second test. The pulse direction, duration, state of charge, and temperature must match.

Estimate current EIS

The method estimates the real and imaginary parts of the later EIS spectrum on that fixed grid.

Estimate SOH

It feeds the estimated EIS, not measured current EIS, into its chained SOH model.

State uncertainty or refuse

Each output includes a development-calibrated 90% interval. Inputs outside the frozen domain are refused.

If a complete current EIS spectrum is supplied, V5 can also run a separate measured-EIS-to-SOH comparison. A partial current spectrum is refused.

What V5 improved

Earlier versions could use a measured current EIS spectrum when estimating SOH. That does not match the intended missing-measurement use case. V5 fixes this development mismatch: it trains the SOH chain using predicted EIS from other cells, adds 90% uncertainty intervals, freezes its inputs and model, and refuses out-of-domain rows instead of guessing.

What internal development showed

On a nested leave-one-cell-out test, V5 trained on other Leonardi cells and kept one complete cell out at a time. The chained predicted-EIS-to-SOH path passed all 8 held-out cells. Its mean relative improvement over the baseline was 83.3%; its weakest held-out cell still improved by 71.4%.

This is useful development evidence because the SOH model did not receive measured current EIS. It is still evidence from one development family, not proof that the method transfers to other batteries.

What the SiCWell external test showed

V5 was frozen before SiCWell was inspected. The test joined 892 rows from 223 checkups across 26 cells. The unchanged domain guard accepted 0 rows and abstained from 892. With no accepted rows, there is no released external accuracy score.

The fresh SiCWell EIS shapes were far outside the frozen development domain, especially the normalized real impedance from 0.5 to 20 Hz. The frozen distance threshold was 0.406; even the nearest SiCWell row was outside it. The guard did its job by refusing to pretend that an out-of-domain prediction was trustworthy.

What this does not prove

  • It does not show that V5 works on SiCWell or on batteries in general.
  • It does not turn estimated EIS or SOH into observed measurements.
  • It does not justify widening the guard after seeing SiCWell.
  • It does not make SiCWell reusable as a clean V5 validation set.

What would be needed next

A future V6 could use SiCWell as development data, but it would need a different untouched external dataset. That kept-back test must link the same cell across fresh and later EIS, matched pulse resistance, and measured capacity or SOH. The V6 model, baseline, acceptance gates, and domain guard must be frozen before it is scored.
