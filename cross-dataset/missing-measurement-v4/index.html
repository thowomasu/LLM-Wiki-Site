Version 4: Scale-Normalized External Test

A self-contained technical account of the V4 inputs, equations, Extra Trees models, frozen Oxford test, results, and limits.

Start here: Version 4 in everyday language

Imagine measuring a battery when it is fresh, ageing it, and then running one short current pulse. Version 4 uses the fresh EIS measurement and the change in pulse resistance to estimate the battery's current EIS. A separate model then estimates health, but that health model still receives the real current EIS measurement.

Known from the fresh cellThe starting EIS spectrum at the same state of charge.
Cheap new measurementA 10-second pulse gives a resistance-change signal.
PredictedThe current real and imaginary EIS values at nine frequencies.
Still measuredThe current EIS used by the Version 4 health model.
EIS spectrumA list of battery impedance values measured across several frequencies.
Real and imaginary EISTwo numerical parts of the same impedance measurement. Both must be predicted.
NormalizationRescaling values so large-impedance cells do not dominate smaller-impedance cells.
Prediction headOne model responsible for one output, such as real EIS, imaginary EIS, or SOH.
The important limitation:

Version 4 did not complete the missing-EIS-to-health task. It predicted EIS, but its health result still depended on measured current EIS.

Executive Read

Version 4 asked whether impedance-scale normalization could move one model to a different commercial NCA cell dataset. It used eight Leonardi cells for development. The model, simple comparisons, and pass rules were then locked before the final Oxford answers were viewed. Three Oxford cells were scored once.

What the model sees

Fresh same-cell EIS, state of charge, and the percentage increase in 10-second pulse resistance.

What it predicts

Changes in real and negative-imaginary EIS on nine frequencies. A separate health head uses measured current EIS.

What passed

Both EIS heads and the measured-EIS-to-SOH head beat their frozen baselines under both defensible Oxford frequency mappings.

What it does not prove

It does not prove the complete missing-EIS chain because the SOH head still receives measured current EIS.

Verdict:

Limited external support for the exact Oxford NCA test: three cells, 24 C, 50% SOC, and the frozen Version 4 contract.

Pipeline

1. Fresh referenceSame-cell EIS at the same SOC becomes the carry-forward baseline.
2. Pulse changeA matched 5 to 10 second pulse gives relative resistance increase.
3. Normalize scaleThe 1 Hz baseline magnitude separates cell size from spectral shape.
4. Predict EIS changeTwo Extra Trees heads predict normalized real and imaginary deltas.
5. Reconstruct EISAdd the predicted scaled delta back to the fresh spectrum.
6. Predict SOHA separate Extra Trees head uses measured current EIS at 50% SOC.
The key V4 change:

V3 struggled when absolute impedance scale changed. V4 removed absolute scale from the model features and predicted relative spectral change instead.

Math

1. Reference impedance scale at 1 Hz
\[s = \sqrt{B_{\mathrm{re},1}^2 + B_{-\mathrm{im},1}^2}\]
Bs

B is the fresh same-cell baseline spectrum. The scalar s removes the large absolute-size difference between cell formats.

2. Normalized baseline shape and training target
\[b_k = \frac{B_k}{s}, \qquad \Delta z_k = \frac{Z_k - B_k}{s}\]
b_kB_ksdelta_z_kZ_k

The model predicts the change from fresh baseline, not the absolute aged spectrum.

3. EIS reconstruction
\[\widehat{Z}_k = B_k + s\, f_k\!\left(\mathrm{SOC},\Delta R_{\mathrm{int}},b_1,\ldots,b_{18}\right)\]
Z_hat_kB_ksf_kdelta_R_int
4. Pulse resistance and SOH
\[R_{10s} = \left|\frac{V(t_0+10)-V(t_0)}{\widetilde{I}_{\mathrm{pulse}}}\right|,\quad \Delta R_{\mathrm{int}}(\%)=100\frac{R_{\mathrm{now}}-R_{\mathrm{fresh}}}{R_{\mathrm{fresh}}},\quad \mathrm{SOH}(\%)=100\frac{Q_{\mathrm{now}}}{Q_{\mathrm{fresh}}}\]
R_10sdelta_R_intQ_nowQ_fresh
5. Scoring and improvement gate
\[\mathrm{typical prediction error}=\sqrt{\frac1N\sum_i(y_i-\widehat{y}_i)^2},\qquad I=\frac{E_{\mathrm{baseline}}-E_{\mathrm{model}}}{E_{\mathrm{baseline}}}\]

EIS uses pooled typical prediction error. SOH uses pooled MAE. Every head must satisfy I ≥ 0.05.

Both mappings passed. The result is positive external evidence, but the independent sample is still only three cells.

Limits and limit of the evidence

  • The external result covers one exact Oxford protocol, one NCA cell model, three cells, one temperature, and one SOC.
  • The SOH head uses measured current EIS. V4 does not solve the full missing-EIS-to-SOH problem.
  • The model is not a generic NMC or generic cylindrical-cell model.
  • The 5% gate is a predeclared comparison threshold, not proof of clinical or operational usefulness.
Correct claim:

V4 has narrow support from one locked outside-data test under the exact Oxford rules. It does not show that the method works for every battery or laboratory.
